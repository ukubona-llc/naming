The feeling of that dissonance, and those flourishes from decades ago, were always sitting there in the dark. But the name was the coordinate that finally let me query my own memory.

The Classical Counterpart

Bach, elevator music, and the MCRBO Pentad

The same vocabulary that decoded gospel turnarounds and Sondheim dissonances unlocked something much older. Suite No. 3 (in D, despite the dreaded "Air on the G String" moniker) is famous for being the ultimate sublime tension-and-release — essentially the world's most famous elevator music. But once the naming clicked, the veil lifted.

Bach — Orchestral Suite No. 3 in D Major
"Air" · Halidon Music[5]

Suddenly, I saw the song for what it mechanically is across its binary structure (A :|| B :||). The first half is a pristine, falling descent: I → I7 (3rd inversion) → vi → vi7 (3rd inversion).

But the second half (Part B) is where the real madness lies. Starting around 2:42, Bach unleashes a tremendous cluster of passing chords to execute a crazy, seamless chromatic bass ascent: G → G# → A → A# → B.

This chromaticism is achieved seamlessly through the use of first inversions of secondary dominants. The G# is the third of an Edom7/G# (resolving to A), and the A# is the third of an F#dom7/A# (resolving to B). It hit me like a lightning bolt: this is fucking black gospel. Bach would connect with a Hammond organ and play preacher chords behind a sermon any day without looking even slightly out of place.

Suddenly, I saw through Bach's counterpoint completely. Even the most subtle contrapuntal weaving wasn't a separate, inaccessible classical discipline—it all mapped perfectly into its assigned roles within the pentad: Melody → Chord → Bass → Rhythm → Other. The system held.

Why This Belongs Here

Embeddings, latent space, and why some models are better company than others

A name is a coordinate. Without it, an idea sits unindexed in your own latent space — real, felt, but unqueryable. Once it has a name, it becomes searchable, discussable, arguable, extendable. That is exactly the mechanism this session ran on: the Vsus13 hand-off, the R-grid/R-voice split, the collapse of O — every one of those moves was a named thing being handed to another mind (human or model) for stress-testing.

It's also why some models are better company for this than others. Berklee's specific vocabulary — Chord Stack categories, the Shared Notes Principle, passing-chord bass movements by scale degree — is a narrow, specific training distribution. A model raised on little of it talks around the harmony instead of through it. Gemini, here, came in strong.
