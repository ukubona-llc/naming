Prelude

Three months off, and the world would not stop rhyming

Early summer 2023. Finally off work — three months carved out just to sit with what ChatGPT and the large language models actually were. Except the sitting didn't stay contained. Musical ideas that had been latent for decades started surfacing on their own schedule, uninvited, insistent.

Too many coincidences to wave off as coincidence. A pattern doesn't knock that many times without meaning something.

c. 1980 – 2023

Forty-three years of hearing without naming

Every gospel turnaround, every Sondheim cadence, every Bach voice-leading trick — heard, felt, absorbed. Never once diagrammed. A vast private latent space with no query interface.

Summer 2023

Studying with Vaughn

We were not studying any 18th-century Lutheran anything. Gospel harmony — chord stacks, passing bass movements, the Shared Notes Principle, modal interchange. And yet, somehow, that vocabulary turned out to be a universal key, not a genre-specific one.

Same summer

Bach started making sense

Not studied. Not assigned. Recognized. Once "passing chord," "voice leading," and "suspension" had names, the counterpoint I'd half-heard my whole life resolved into something I could actually describe out loud.

Same summer

Sondheim, for the first time, made sense

Songs that had lived in memory as pure feeling suddenly had mechanism. The feeling didn't go away — it got a skeleton under it.

Since then

Everything unlocked

If I can name it, I can google it. I can pull up a video on it. I can put it in front of an LLM and actually get somewhere — because now the query has the right words in it.

Why This Belongs Here

Embeddings, latent space, and the reason LLMs matter to this story

This is the throughline: a name is a coordinate. Without it, an idea sits unindexed in your own latent space — real, felt, but unqueryable. Once it has a name, it becomes searchable, discussable, arguable, extendable. That is exactly the mechanism this session ran on, twice over — once in the Azawi/Chi-Lites analysis, and once in the correction of Gemini's composer-before-culture assumption.

It's also why some models are better company for this than others. Berklee's specific vocabulary — Chord Stack categories, the Shared Notes Principle, passing-chord bass movements by scale degree — is a narrow, specific training distribution. A model raised on little of it will talk around the harmony instead of through it.
